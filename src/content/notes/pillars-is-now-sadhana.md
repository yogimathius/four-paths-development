The app was called Pillars for most of its life. The name described a structure: five disciplines holding up one platform. By the time the first release shipped, only one of those pillars was in the product, and it was the one people actually opened it for. The name was still describing the plan, not the experience.

That experience is a Hatha yoga practice, so the app now carries the word for it. In Sanskrit, *sādhanā* means practice: something done regularly, in service of something larger than any single session. It is a better promise than a framework. It says what the app is for before saying how it is organized.

A name also shapes the product. Pillars invited a list of features and a grid of destinations. Sadhana leans toward a quieter question: what does the next session need to be? That is the question the named stages, the experience check and the spoken guidance are trying to answer, and it is easier to keep asking under a name that points at the practice.

## Where to find it

Sadhana now has its own website at [sadhanawithin.com](https://sadhanawithin.com), the best place to start if you are curious about the app. It is available on Android through [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pillars.mobile). A seven-day free trial is available, followed by a subscription; the current price and renewal terms are shown in the app before you begin.
